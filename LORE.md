**Status:** approved (with pins below). Ready for systems wiring when staged.  
**Tone north star (GAME_DESIGN.md §9):** bittersweet, weathered, hopeful underneath. Lived-in galaxy. NPCs with agendas. Emotionally legible writing, not generic sci-fi filler.

| Keep (essence) | Do not reuse |

## 1. Protagonist

### Name
**Captain Rin Voss**  
(“Rin” is short, soft, and works in spoken dialogue; “Voss” reads as a small family firm name on old invoices.)

### Role
Independent freighter captain, one generation deep into a failing family trade name. Not a chosen one — a working captain with a sealed question and a ship that still smells like someone else’s coffee.

### Presentation
- **Gender:** male (art and pronouns: he/him). Clear masculine presentation in art — not androgynous.
- **Age:** late 20s (reads young adult, not mid-30s+).

### Personality (dialogue & expression)
- Weathered and grief-touched at the core (Halden’s absence, the sealed page), but the **surface read is cool, relaxed confidence** — competent easygoing rogue energy, not strained cheer or jolly grin.
- Dry humor and ease in dialogue; unbothered and sure of himself until the mission weight shows in quieter beats.
- **Portraits:** steady easy eyes (not raised/worried brows), slight knowing half-smile, relaxed posture — not open-mouthed grinning or pleading warmth.

### Appearance notes (portrait / consistency)
- **Age presentation:** late 20s — short clean hair, no grey, reads young adult not seasoned middle-age.
- **Build:** lean, work-strong; not military bulk; clearly male frame and face.
- **Hair:** short, **fluffy/wavy**, clean well-groomed dark brown-black — volume and soft wave, **no ponytail, no grey**.
- **Face:** clear alert eyes with normal iris color and **direct eye contact** (not glassy/unfocused); faint scar along the left brow (old dock accident); slight knowing half-smile, relaxed brows. Light facial hair (not a full beard): thin **anchor line** from center of lower lip down to chin; light stubble on cheeks/jaw toward mid-cheek (v6 final). Still clearly late 20s.
- **Locked identity art (default):** `assets/characters/rin-voss-reference-v6-wavy-fitted-anchor-stubble.jpg`  
  Further art = **expression/context variants** of this identity (cool dock, serious heritage, tired, banter, etc.), not redesigns of face/outfit/props unless LORE changes.
- **Clothes:** **fitted, well-tailored** flight jacket (worn but sharp, athletic silhouette — not baggy/oversized/hunched). Teal lining and collar accents kept as the Voss/shipboard signature. Plain fitted undershirt; slim practical trousers; boots that have been resoled more than once.
- **Signature props:** (1) small **brass-and-glass transit compass** on a cord (heirloom/story). (2) **sci-fi pilot goggles pushed up on forehead/hair** (cool traveler ID, not worn over eyes).
- **Shipboard color accent:** faded teal lining in the jacket (matches the game’s teal accent palette).
- **Mood in still images:** cool, unbothered confidence on the surface; grief stays underneath and does not need to read on the face in the default portrait.
- **Art note:** LORE still specifies the teal-lined jacket; styling is **fitted pilot cut**, not a gloomy overcoat — identity kept, silhouette updated for “knows his way around.”

### Starting situation (player-facing, no spoilers required)
Rin inherits the battered freighter **Morrowlit** and a stack of half-paid obligations after years of scraping by on short hops. A single sealed page from Halden Voss’s last ledger — coordinates that don’t match any legal chart — is the only personal lead. The rest of the galaxy treats Rin as just another small captain with a soft name and hard numbers.

## 2. Heritage mystery (shape, not full script)

### The official story
Twelve years ago, freighter captain **Halden Voss** — Rin’s **parent** (copy never forces father/mother) — left on a routine **pharm-stock** run from the inner markets toward the Quiet Verge and never docked. Insurers closed the claim as **lost to raiders**. The family firm never recovered; Rin grew up on the leftovers.

### What actually drives the throughline
Halden did not simply vanish into bad luck. The last run was a cover: the hold carried a secondary cargo — **memory-proof canisters** (encrypted witness logs) related to a buried disaster called **the Pale Wake**. A colony habitat lost atmosphere under disputed circumstances; official reports blamed “systems failure.” Halden had been hired, then double-crossed, then tried to get the proof somewhere it couldn’t be bought.

**Bittersweet core:** Halden’s disappearance was partly sacrifice and partly unfinished duty. The player is not hunting a cartoon villain so much as peeling a silence that people in power still prefer closed.

### How truth arrives (milestone-triggered, coordinate-agnostic)
Hand-written story beats fire on player progress, not map locations. Example anchor order (numbers tunable later):

| Beat | Example trigger | Revelation layer |
|---|---|---|
| **Ash** | First profitable run / first 3 stations visited | Someone remembers Halden after the “loss” date — a dockhand, a rumor, a wrong date on a receipt |
| **Ledger** | First ship upgrade or first loan taken | The sealed page decodes further: the cargo wasn’t only pharm stock |
| **Name** | Net worth crosses a modest threshold, or first contraband decision resolved | A faction or firm name surfaces: **Helix Transit** (shipping consortium; public face of the cover-up) |
| **Rival mirror** | First deep encounter or meeting Jex Morrow’s mid-game beat | Another family was burned by the same silence — the rival’s motive becomes personal, not pure greed |
| **Pale Wake** | Mid-late milestones (stations visited + reputation extremes + story flags) | Player learns what the canisters hold: testimony from survivors and a falsified safety report |
| **Capstone** | Final heritage flags complete | Rin learns where Halden’s trail ends — not a pure triumph, not pure tragedy (see resolution shape below) |

### Resolution shape (narrative capstone; game continues after)
The capstone answers **what happened to Halden** and **what Rin does with the truth**. Exact branch text is content work for narrative data later; the **allowed outcomes** are:

1. **The Quiet Truth** — Halden is confirmed gone; left a last message for Rin. Player can release the proof, bury it, or sell leverage. Open trading continues with a settled heart.
2. **The Long Echo** — Halden survived long enough to hide the last canister; Rin recovers it. Same choice set about the proof; slightly more hope in the writing.
3. **The Cost of Light** — Getting the truth damages standing with Helix-aligned Compact officials (reputation hit) but earns lasting goodwill with Verge communities. Trading continues under that new political weather.

No hard game-over. **Stage 9:** epilogue-as-captain’s-log assembles from reputation, crew trust, and the capstone proof choice (release / bury / sell); path shape (Quiet Truth / Long Echo / Cost of Light) is inferred from playthrough weather. Trading continues after “Keep flying.”

## 3. Setting frame (one paragraph)

The game takes place in a settled but uneven arm of human-and-allied space nicknamed **the Lantern Marches** — a string of trade regions bound more by fuel stops and old treaties than by any grand empire. Power is soft: contracts, reputation, and who will still sell you coolant when you’re late on interest. The stars at the edge feel empty on purpose.

---

## 4. Species & factions (§3.7)

Four factions. Each is both a **political identity** and a **cultural flavor** for station ownership, tavern tone, and encounter tables. Reputation range in systems: **−100 to +100**.

### 4.1 The Compact
- **Who:** Human-majority trade federation and port authority network; the “respectable” face of the Marches.
- **Flavor:** Contracts, inspections, clean corridors, polite threats. They keep the lanes open and the paperwork heavier than the cargo.
- **Stations feel like:** Bright signage, security drones, fair-but-stiff markets.
- **Reputation effects (design intent):** better prices and fewer hostile “inspection” encounters when positive; more fines, holds, and cold tavern dialogue when negative.
- **Story role:** institutional gravity around Helix Transit; not cartoon-evil, but allergic to scandals.

### 4.2 Veshari Confluence
- **Who:** The **Veshari** — tall, fine-boned, feather-crested humanoids with iridescent neck plumage that flattens when they lie (or when they *want* you to think they’re lying).
- **Flavor:** Hospitality as strategy. Gossip is currency. A good story opens better rates than a hard bargain.
- **Stations feel like:** Warm light, layered fabrics, music you hear through bulkheads.
- **Story role:** best tavern rumor ecology; neutral brokers who may know more about Halden’s last drinks than any archive.

### 4.3 Korr Forgeholds
- **Who:** The **Korr** — broad, silicate-plated people who look half-carved; voices like gravel in a drum. Live long; hold grudges and favors with equal precision.
- **Flavor:** Shipwrights, ore barons, “fix it or scrap it.” Sentiment is rare; reliability is sacred.
- **Stations feel like:** Heat, clang, honest pricing, ugly-but-immortal architecture.
- **Story role:** ship upgrades and industrial goods; they respect Rin’s name if Rin deals clean.

### 4.4 Veil Freeholds
- **Who:** Mixed species fringe polities (human, Veshari expats, Korr outcasts, others) who refuse Compact registry. Not a hive mind — a patchwork of docks that share a motto: *no ledger without a face*.
- **Flavor:** Dangerous edges, better music, worse insurance. Contraband-adjacent without being pure pirate.
- **Stations feel like:** Improvised lighting, layered paint, dogs of unknown species asleep on cargo nets.
- **Story role:** scavenge adjacency, soft landing when Compact heat rises; early warmth toward the Voss name.

**Note:** “Species” and “faction” mostly align for Veshari/Korr; Compact and Veil are multi-species polities. Dialogue pools should be **species-aware** when an NPC is Veshari or Korr, and **polity-aware** for Compact/Veil tone.

## 5. Galaxy layout (first playable version)

**4 regions · 12 trade stations · 3 scavenge sites**  
Procedural generation later may shuffle positions/prices/connectivity; **names and regional identities below are the v1 content set**.

### Region A — Lantern Reach *(starter, safest)*
Compact-leaning commerce spine. Good tutorial market. Home dock energy.

| Station | Owner flavor | Economic lean |
|---|---|---|
| **Haven Spindle** | Compact | Balanced hub; player start; tavern + moneylender present |
| **Glassmarket** | Veshari | Luxury + rumor-rich; strong on finished goods |
| **Twin Dock** | Compact / mixed | Fuel-cheap connector; modest everything |

### Region B — Ashline Corridor *(industrial)*
Korr heartland. Ore out, tech and weapons in.

| Station | Owner flavor | Economic lean |
|---|---|---|
| **Forgewell** | Korr | Bulk ore cheap; meds & luxuries expensive |
| **Cinder Port** | Korr | Ship parts, weapons; rough tavern |
| **Keel Yard** | Korr / Compact treaty dock | Ship upgrades available; pricier fuel |

### Region C — The Quiet Verge *(frontier)*
Sparse, hopeful, under-supplied. Story gravity without forcing coordinates.

| Station | Owner flavor | Economic lean |
|---|---|---|
| **Last Light** | Veil | Meds scarce/expensive; good sell for pharm stocks |
| **Driftgarden** | Veshari outpost | Lumen seeds & food culture; soft hospitality |
| **Pale Harbor** | Mixed / quiet Compact remnant | Name echoes the Pale Wake; subdued, watchful markets |

### Region D — Redwake Expanse *(dangerous edge)*
High margin, high encounter weight. Contraband culture without pure chaos.

| Station | Owner flavor | Economic lean |
|---|---|---|
| **Cutlass Node** | Veil | Weapons & ghost silk traffic; inspections common on the way in |
| **Blacktide** | Veil | Best contraband margins; worst “respectable” reputation optics |
| **Needlepoint** | Independent / contested | Thin markets, sharp people; good for dumping heat |

### Scavenge sites (§3.8 flavor)
Not trade stations. Finite salvage pools; risk of rival scavengers / unstable wrecks.

| Site | Theme | What you pull | Hazard flavor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Bone Arc** | Old convoy graveyard | Scrap, circuit weave, rare ship parts | Unstable wreck physics; claim-jumpers |
| **Silent Lattice** | Abandoned research web | Tech components, odd “clean” salvage | Radiation ghosts / automated defenses (text, not twitch) |
| **Hullgrave** | Half-crushed freighter cluster | Mixed cargo leftovers, occasional contraband canisters | Rival captains; oxygen lies |

**Regeneration intent:** slow recovery over turns so revisiting matters; never a free infinite tap.

## 6. Commodities (v1 list)

Eight goods. Believable spread; one clear contraband lane. Base prices are **design targets** for the home region average — actual station prices layer supply/demand + noise.

| ID | Name | Legal? | Base price (cr) | Typical buy-low / sell-high regions | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ore` | **Bulk Ore** | Yes | 12 | Buy Ashline → sell Lantern / Redwake | Workhorse bulk filler |
| `pharm` | **Pharm Stock** | Yes | 40 | Buy Lantern → sell Quiet Verge | Story-tinged staple; Halden’s cover cargo |
| `circuits` | **Circuit Weave** | Yes | 55 | Buy Lantern/Quiet salvage → sell Ashline | Tech components |
| `luxury` | **Silkglass** | Yes | 90 | Buy Glassmarket → sell frontier / industrial | Fragile prestige goods |
| `arms` | **Armaments** | Restricted | 70 | Buy Ashline → sell Redwake / Verge | Semi-legal; can color inspection tone |
| `seeds` | **Lumen Seeds** | Yes | 28 | Buy Driftgarden → sell industrial stations | Food/agri biotech; hopeful flavor |
| `coolant` | **Deep Coolant** | Yes | 22 | Buy industrial → sell anywhere hot-running | Utility good; always quietly useful |
| `ghost_silk` | **Ghost Silk** | **Contraband** | 120 | Buy Redwake → sell Core if you dare | High margin; spikes inspection / deep-encounter risk |

**Cargo fantasy:** Ghost Silk is smuggled ceremonial fabric laced with banned stimulants — beautiful, illegal, and exactly the kind of thing that makes Compact clerks smile without warmth.

### Starting economy sketch (pacing, not final balance)
- **Starting credits:** ~800–1200 after tutorial debt framing (tunable).
- **Starting debt option:** moneylender available immediately at Haven Spindle; first loan soft, interest climbs if ignored (no hard fail — terms worsen, forced cargo sales, temporary market cold-shoulder).
- **Fuel:** priced so short hops are comfortable and cross-region runs require planning.
- **Goal feel:** early game = “make the ship breathe”; mid = “routes and reputation”; late = “story and empire.”

## 7. Recurring NPCs

### 7.1 Madame Sera Quill — *Moneylender* (Haven Spindle)
- **Species/faction:** Human; Compact-adjacent but independent operator.
- **Appearance notes:** silvered hair in a precise coil; dark tailored coat; ink-stained fingertips; always seated as if the chair is a throne she rented fairly.
- **Personality:** soft-spoken, surgically polite, never performs anger. Treats debt as a **relationship with rules**. Will extend a first kindness because she remembers Halden’s ledger discipline — and will name the interest increase like a weather report.
- **Agenda:** keep small captains circulating (dead clients don’t pay). Not a secret boss of the mystery; she **knows more than she sells** and unlocks lines as Rin’s net worth / story flags rise.
- **Hook line (voice sample):** “I don’t break people, Captain Voss. I keep them in motion. Motion is how interest lives.”

### 7.2 Pax Renn — *Tavern keeper* (The Last Honest Cup, Haven Spindle)
- **Species/faction:** Human; culturally Veil-friendly, lives under Compact light.
- **Appearance notes:** broad, flour-dusted forever; apron over an old ship-knit sweater; laugh lines that arrived early.
- **Personality:** warm, nosy, protective. Talks like a retired cook who has heard every lie twice. Serves terrible coffee that somehow tastes like someone tried.
- **Agenda:** keep the room human. Sells rumors that are **directionally true** more often than precise. Will gently push Rin toward rest when the numbers get ugly.
- **Hook line:** “You look like your parent before a long run. Sit. Money hears better when you’re not shaking.”

*(Other stations can reuse the “tavern keeper” role with local names from variation pools; Pax is the **recurring face** of the hub.)*

### 7.3 Captain Jex Morrow — *Rival*
- **Species/faction:** Human; flies Veil colors when convenient, Compact manners when profitable.
- **Appearance notes:** short copper hair; expensive jacket mended invisibly well (dandy silhouette); decorative sidearm. **Two approved looks** (not mutually exclusive):
  - **Default UI / cast tone with Rin:** brighter sharp smirk (`jex-morrow-portrait-v1-charming`) — rival as peer energy, cool and cunning without cartoon evil.
  - **Heritage / wound moments:** heavier eyes, grief under the dandy cut (`jex-morrow-portrait-v2-heavier`) — the LORE truth when the sibling/Helix debt is in focus.
- **Personality:** charming and competitive on the surface; **grief/circumstance shaped him**, not innate cartoon antagonism. Sibling lost to Helix-tied work; wants proof for leverage *and* grief. Needles Rin with competence, not cruelty. Default face can smirk; story face can weight.
- **Agenda:** Jex’s **sibling** was on a Helix-contracted crew tied to the Pale Wake silence. Jex wants the proof for leverage and for grief. Can become antagonist, reluctant ally, or both across a playthrough.
- **Relationship to systems:** can appear in tavern vignettes, quick encounters (race for a salvage claim), and at least one deep encounter branch.
- **Hook line:** “Don’t look wounded, Voss. We’re both flying the same unpaid debt. Yours just has a prettier name.”
